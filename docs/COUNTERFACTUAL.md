# Path to Approval (synthetic demonstration)

`POST /api/v1/applications/{id}/path-to-approval` searches prospective actions for referred or declined applications. It returns up to three distinct policy accepted plans: Fastest, Cheapest and Strongest. Each plan includes bilingual steps, original and target values, a timeline, predicted probability of default (PD), score change, indicative approved amount, and lender policy checks. `POST /api/v1/applications/{id}/what-if` scores slider changes without changing historical evidence. The lender and applicant use the same champion model, challenger model and versioned policy.

The search uses a bounded four-step beam over a discrete grid. It considers request reduction, longer tenor, maintained balance, digital sales, improved bill punctuality over three months, NTN registration, added verifiable collateral and sustained expense savings. Each action maps to an actionable registry entry and observes its bounds, effort weight and time to effect. Amount and tenor recompute instalment, DSCR and loan-to-revenue ratios; digital share also recomputes cash share; collateral coverage updates with amount. Vintage, bureau history, delinquency, protected attributes and missingness flags are never changed. A plan is returned only when the real policy says APPROVE and champion PD is at most 9.5%, leaving a 0.5 percentage-point margin below the D-tier referral boundary. Plans are prospective scenarios, not evidence of completed actions or guaranteed offers.

## Measured holdout coverage

Run `python -m app.ml.evaluate_counterfactual` from `backend` to reproduce the full business-disjoint test-split check. On the 600 untouched synthetic test applications, 522 were referred or declined. A plan was found for 485 (92.9%). All 1,452 returned plans were reapplied through the what-if scoring and policy path and returned APPROVE with the PD margin. The evaluation took 117.9 seconds on the development machine. The 37 without a plan are reported honestly; hard non-actionable policy blocks and insufficient feasible improvement can prevent approval. These figures measure synthetic scenario feasibility, not real-world borrower uptake or approval rates.
